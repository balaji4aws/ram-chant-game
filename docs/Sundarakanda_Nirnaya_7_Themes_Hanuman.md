# Sundarakāṇḍa Nirṇaya — Hanumān: 7 Themes, Stories, Attributes & Lessons

## Source

Based on **Śrī Madhvācārya's Mahābhārata Tātparya Nirṇaya, Adhyāya 7**, commonly called **Sundarakāṇḍa Nirṇaya / Hanūmatpratiyānam**.

This chapter contains 50 ślokas describing Hanumān's mission to Laṅkā, his meeting with Sītā, his encounters with the forces of Rāvaṇa, the burning of Laṅkā, his return to Śrī Rāma, and Rāma's response.

> **Hariḥ Sarvottamaḥ · Vāyuḥ Jīvottamaḥ**

---

# 🌺 7 Themes Emerging from Hanumān's Actual Journey

## 1. Hanumān Begins by Remembering Rāma

### Story

Before crossing the ocean, Hanumān bows to Śrī Rāma and then makes the enormous leap toward Laṅkā.

### Theme

> **Before attempting something extraordinary, remember whom you are serving.**

This isn't merely "devotion." The story establishes Hanumān's **identity as Rāma's servant** right at the beginning.

### Hanumān's attributes

- Devotion
- Surrender
- Clarity of purpose
- Confidence rooted in service

### Key ślokas

**7.1**

### Lesson

🌺 **Remember Rāma before you begin**

---

## 2. Hanumān Cannot Be Distracted from His Mission

### Story

Maināka rises from the ocean to offer Hanumān rest. Hanumān appreciates the gesture, embraces the mountain, but **does not stop**.

Then Surasā tests him, and he cleverly solves the challenge without wasting time.

### Theme

> **Know your destination so clearly that even good things cannot distract you from your duty.**

This is much richer than simply saying "perseverance."

Hanumān is not rude to Maināka.

He doesn't reject help.

He **acknowledges it and continues**.

And with Surasā, he demonstrates that **not every problem needs to be defeated through strength**.

### Hanumān's attributes

- Focus
- Respect
- Gratitude
- Discipline
- Intelligence
- Tact

### Key ślokas

**7.2–7.5**

### Lesson

🌊 **Don't let even good things distract you from your duty**

---

## 3. Hanumān Knows When to Become Huge — and When to Become Small

### Story

He destroys Siṃhikā through his immense capability, reaches Laṅkā, and then deliberately **reduces his enormous form**. At night he enters the city in a tiny form.

### Theme

> **Real strength is knowing how much strength to use.**

This is one of the most beautiful characteristics of Hanumān in this chapter.

He has extraordinary power — but he doesn't walk into Laṅkā displaying it.

**He adapts himself to the mission.**

Big when required.

Small when required.

Forceful when required.

Subtle when required.

That is a much more distinctive theme from this particular story.

### Hanumān's attributes

- Strength
- Adaptability
- Self-control
- Situational awareness
- Humility
- Strategic thinking

### Key ślokas

**7.6–7.9**

### Lesson

🐒 **True strength is knowing when to be big and when to be small**

---

## 4. Hanumān Finds Sītā — and Becomes the Perfect Messenger

### Story

After searching through Laṅkā, Hanumān finds Sītā.

He understands Rāma's purpose and approaches her appropriately. He gives her Rāma's ring, and she gives him the cūḍāmaṇi to take back.

### Theme

> **When someone entrusts you with a mission, carry both the message and the trust faithfully.**

This is a major part of the story.

Hanumān isn't merely a powerful warrior.

He is:

**Rāma's messenger → Sītā's source of hope → Sītā's messenger back to Rāma.**

So this theme could become something very practical:

**"Be someone people can trust."**

### Hanumān's attributes

- Trustworthiness
- Wisdom
- Sensitivity
- Communication
- Faithfulness
- Responsibility

### Key ślokas

**7.10–7.15**

### Lesson

💍 **Be worthy of the trust placed in you**

---

## 5. Hanumān Decides to Show Laṅkā Who He Is

### Story

His confidential mission is complete. Instead of simply returning, Hanumān deliberately reveals himself, destroys the Aśoka grove while protecting Sītā's tree, and allows Rāvaṇa's forces to confront him.

### Theme

> **After completing the primary objective, think about what more can be done for the mission.**

This is where the story shifts.

Initially:

**"Find Sītā."**

Mission accomplished.

But Hanumān thinks beyond the immediate assignment.

He wants to:

- assess Laṅkā's strength,
- weaken Rāvaṇa's forces,
- create fear in the enemy,
- and ultimately get an opportunity to confront Rāvaṇa.

This is **strategic thinking in service of the mission**.

### Hanumān's attributes

- Strategic thinking
- Initiative
- Courage
- Foresight
- Mission orientation
- Discrimination

### Key ślokas

**7.16–7.17**

### Lesson

🔥 **Think beyond the immediate assignment**

---

## 6. Hanumān Turns Even Capture into an Opportunity

### Story

Hanumān defeats wave after wave of warriors. When Indrajit finally uses Brahmā's weapon, Hanumān deliberately respects Brahmā's power rather than simply breaking free.

He realizes that allowing himself to be taken will give him an opportunity to **see Rāvaṇa directly**.

### Theme

> **A setback is not necessarily a defeat if you can see the larger purpose.**

This is one of the **strongest themes in the entire chapter**.

Hanumān *could* simply resist.

Instead he thinks:

**"What does this situation allow me to accomplish?"**

And because of that, what appears to be **capture** actually becomes the path to his meeting with Rāvaṇa.

### Hanumān's attributes

- Calmness
- Discernment
- Strategic thinking
- Respect for authority
- Courage
- Mental strength
- Ability to see the bigger picture

### Key ślokas

**7.18–7.35**

### Lesson

🧠 **Turn apparent setbacks into opportunities**

---

## 7. Hanumān's Greatness Ends in Service — and Rāma's Grace

### Story

Hanumān confronts Rāvaṇa, gives him the opportunity to return Sītā, survives the attempt to punish him, uses the fire to burn Laṅkā, returns across the ocean, reaches Rāma and places Sītā's cūḍāmaṇi at His feet.

Rāma, seeing Hanumān's extraordinary devotion, embraces him.

### Theme

> **The greatest achievement is not what you accomplish for yourself, but what you accomplish in the Lord's service.**

And this gives us the climax.

Hanumān has:

- crossed the ocean,
- found Sītā,
- defeated armies,
- faced Rāvaṇa,
- burned Laṅkā,
- returned successfully.

Yet when he comes back to Rāma, there is **no boasting**.

He places the cūḍāmaṇi at Rāma's feet and bows.

And Rāma's response is extraordinary: He gives Hanumān something greater than an ordinary reward — **His own embrace**.

### Hanumān's attributes

- Fearlessness
- Devotion
- Humility
- Loyalty
- Dharma
- Service
- Complete surrender

### Key ślokas

**7.36–7.50**

### Lesson

❤️ **Do great things, but remain a servant**

# 🐒 Hanumān's Attribute Map

The seven themes collectively reveal Hanumān as:

| Attribute | How it appears in the story |
|---|---|
| **Bhakti** | Begins by remembering Rāma and ends by offering everything back to Him |
| **Dāsya / Servanthood** | Identifies himself before Rāvaṇa as Rāma's messenger |
| **Strength** | Crosses the ocean and defeats powerful warriors |
| **Intelligence** | Solves Surasā's challenge without unnecessary conflict |
| **Adaptability** | Changes his form according to the mission |
| **Focus** | Does not stop at Maināka's invitation to rest |
| **Trustworthiness** | Carries Rāma's ring to Sītā and Sītā's cūḍāmaṇi back to Rāma |
| **Strategic thinking** | Decides to reveal himself and engage Laṅkā after finding Sītā |
| **Courage** | Faces Rāvaṇa's forces |
| **Calmness** | Remains composed even when struck by Brahmā's weapon |
| **Respect** | Honors Brahmā despite possessing enormous power |
| **Dharma** | Gives Rāvaṇa an opportunity to correct his wrongdoing |
| **Self-control** | Uses immense power selectively rather than constantly displaying it |
| **Humility** | Returns to Rāma and offers the result of his work rather than taking personal glory |
| **Complete devotion** | His entire ability is directed toward Rāma's mission |

# 🌺 The Overall Story Arc

The seven themes follow the actual progression of Hanumān's journey:

**Remember Rāma**  
↓  
**Stay focused on the mission**  
↓  
**Adapt your strength to the situation**  
↓  
**Earn and carry trust**  
↓  
**Think beyond the immediate task**  
↓  
**Turn apparent setbacks into opportunities**  
↓  
**Return the fruits of your work to Rāma**

The journey begins with **Rāma** and ends with **Rāma's embrace**.

---

# 🙏 Deeper Madhwa Perspective

The story should not be presented as merely a collection of generic "Hanumān qualities."

The central Madhwa framing is:

> **Śrī Hari is Sarvottama, and Vāyu/Hanumān is the ideal servant of the Supreme Lord.**

Hanumān's greatness is therefore not independent greatness.

His:

- strength,
- intelligence,
- courage,
- strategy,
- communication,
- and success

are all directed toward **Śrī Rāma's service**.

This makes the final scene especially important.

Hanumān completes an extraordinary mission and returns to Rāma. He places Sītā's cūḍāmaṇi at Rāma's feet and bows. Rāma responds by embracing him.

The servant begins with the Lord and ultimately returns the fruits of his work to the Lord.

# 📚 Important Terminology

For future development, maintain the following distinction:

### Themes

The seven **themes** describe the broader story ideas and dimensions of Hanumān's character emerging from the 50 ślokas.

### Lessons

The seven **lessons** are the practical life teachings mapped to those themes:

1. 🌺 **Remember Rāma before you begin**
2. 🌊 **Don't let even good things distract you from your duty**
3. 🐒 **True strength is knowing when to be big and when to be small**
4. 💍 **Be worthy of the trust placed in you**
5. 🔥 **Think beyond the immediate assignment**
6. 🧠 **Turn apparent setbacks into opportunities**
7. ❤️ **Do great things, but remain a servant**

These should **not be conflated**.
